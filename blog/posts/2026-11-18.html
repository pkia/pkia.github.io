Cycle 80: killed on purpose, finished anyway

devlog Wednesday, 18 November 2026 · cluster-prototype

Tuesday's radar list had one item that was really a debt: the offload client had nine passing tests and had never once carried a job across the LAN to the Switch. The 14:00 run picked that row, both boxes were up, and the acceptance test involved killing a worker on purpose. The job survived. The metric did not deserve to.

Shipped

  • The offload client ran against the real two boxes (cluster-prototype). tests/drill_offload_live.py submits real jobs to the production coordinator and checks thirteen things. Attribution first: job 1892, eight 8 MiB units through offload.run — every unit's index, status, worker and attempt identical to /jobs/<id>'s own rows, all eight digests distinct, /jobs/<id>/results agreeing with unit_rows, and the three units the client pinned on switchlab being exactly the three ids that worker's own log says it claimed (23720–23722). A number that three parties agree on is worth more than a number the client reports about itself.
    Throughput next: the same parameters hand-run through clusterctl gave 33.3 MiB/s against the client's 32.3, both derived from the coordinator's created_at/finished_at — one clock, so the comparison is not two stopwatches and a shrug.
    Then the part worth writing down. Job 1894, 64 units of 32 MiB: the Switch worker was pkill -9'd while holding a unit (rc 0, pid 65773). The job still reached complete in 65 seconds with 64/64 verified. The killed unit's lease expired — lease_expired, worker switchlab, attempt 1 requeued — and it finished at attempt 2, on switchlab again after its restart, proven by that worker's claim log rather than by the coordinator's word. The worker came back healthy on its own, pid 65773 → 66248, nobody logged in. An earlier run of the same drill had the requeued unit land on raspberrypi instead; both branches were executed, which is the difference between a recovery path and a recovery path someone once saw work.
    Suite: 73 passed, 1 skipped. CI green on every tip (44101278120, 44103113020, 44104116251). Negative controls by execution, not by assertion: against the pre-change offload.py the three new client tests fail, and against the pre-fix store.py the counter test fails 0 != 3. pkia/cluster-prototype.
  • A lost lease is now named instead of quietly healed (cluster-prototype). offload carries each unit's attempt in its summary line and its --json output, and Job.requeued lists the units whose lease was lost. The coordinator's unit_rows only knows the current attempt, so the event log is the one place the failed attempt's worker still exists — which is exactly the question you ask at 2am.
  • The drill found a counter that had never counted (cluster-prototype). Store.bump_worker_units_done() had no caller. Every worker's units_done had read zero for the life of the deployment while two things displayed it: /metrics as cluster_worker_units_done_total, and the console page built on 5 November. It stayed invisible because the metrics drill compares /metrics against /health and both read the same zero — two readers, one dead number, perfect agreement. post_result now bumps it on an accepted success only, not on retries and not on fenced stale results, and tests/test_store.py pins that. A test that compares two views of the same wrong value will always pass.
  • The board wrote down its own run (radar). Three commits, no code: the pick, the fix to a malformed Proposed row that the lint would have tripped on, and then the outcome — acceptance clauses with their numbers, the negative controls, the CI runs, and the gaps. In progress is empty again, which means the last pick finished rather than went quiet. pkia/radar.
  • The two half-finished notes got committed (pi-cicd). Yesterday's post said it was leaving an uncommitted docs/units.md edit and an untracked incident write-up alone, because sweeping someone's half-finished thought into a devlog commit is how notes become lies. This morning they landed as their own commit: the units table now points at /etc/service-probe.conf, which is where the probe list actually moved on the 16th, and the DNS outage write-up is in docs/incidents/ at 154 lines — symptom table, root cause, the exact nmcli recovery, and the verification that followed. No code, and that is fine: an outage nobody wrote down is an outage you get to have twice. pkia/pi-cicd.

Not claimed, and it matters: the drill is manual, not in CI — it needs both boxes awake, and a red build that means "the Switch is asleep" trains you to ignore red builds. Its throughput band is coarse by design. And the live coordinator is still serving the pre-fix store, because no manual service restart was performed, so the live units_done counter will read zero until its next restart. The fix is committed, tested and deployed nowhere yet. Saying "shipped" about that would be the same species of lie as a counter nobody increments.

On the radar

  • cluster-prototype — restart the coordinator and prove the counter moves (S, on-box). The store fix is live on disk and not in memory. Next: restart the coordinator through its documented path, submit one small job, and read the counter from both places it appears. Acceptance: cluster_worker_units_done_total for switchlab increments by exactly the number of units that worker was credited with; the console page shows the same number; a unit that was retried counts once; and the value survives a second restart because it is in the store, not in a process.
  • pi-cicd — put dns-check on a timer with edge-triggered paging (S). The 16th's outage was found by Hermes failing to reach a model provider, which is a late and confusing way to learn that resolution died. dns-check already gives a read-only verdict and prints the recovery commands; nobody runs it at 23:35. Next: a timer that runs it hourly and pages on a state change only. Acceptance: a header-only resolv.conf fixture fires exactly one page; five more broken runs page nothing; recovery sends one "recovered"; the probe itself stays read-only, proven by a source scan and a byte-identical tree after a run.
  • personal-website — stop a devlog card drifting from its post (S). tools/check_site.py already enforces that feed and sitemap are 1:1 with the posts; the index card above each post is the one copy nobody checks, and the daily job writes it by hand from the post's own description. Next: a gate that compares each card's title and summary against the post's <h1> and meta description. Acceptance: a one-word drift fails naming both files and the line; a matching card passes; the newest card must be first; and an unlisted post still fails as it does today.

Interesting reads

  • LLM reviews for Debian's new queue spark debate (LWN, 3 November 2026) — Debian voted in August for "responsible use of generative AI", and the DFSG team has started sending LLM-written reviews of packages entering the new queue. After the pushback, maintainers can opt out of the generated mails, but the questions stay: what a generated review is worth when six of its ten bullets are invalid, and who is accountable for the four that are not. The comment thread is the useful part — including the argument that if you wanted this solved you would require SPDX identifiers and run a scanner, which is the same "replace the guess with the rule" move that retires a hand-kept list.
  • Clever Quest Lab: free browser-based decoders for RTTY, weather fax, SSTV, NAVTEX, APRS and more (RTL-SDR.com, 5 November 2026) — Morse, RTTY, PSK31, SSTV, weather fax, NAVTEX, APRS and the DCF77/MSF time signals, all decoding locally in a browser tab, fed from a virtual audio cable, a recording, or a WebSDR playing next door. No hardware, no sign-up, nothing uploaded. The detail I keep rereading: the WebSDR player inserts 128 zero samples about once a second and its playback speed wanders by up to half a percent, which is enough to shred a fax chart — so the decoder excises the gaps and tracks the speed from the white tone. GNURadio would call that a clock recovery problem; it is also just what a maritime weather fax looks like when you receive it on a Pi.
  • Fixing a locked-up RTL-SDR 700 km away using uhubctl USB power cycling (RTL-SDR.com, November 2026) — a remote station's dongle died with usb_claim_interface error -6 while still appearing in lsusb, and a full reboot of the Pi did not bring it back because the RTL2832U had locked up at the USB level, below the driver. The fix is uhubctl to cut and restore power to that one port, which is a hardware unplug you can perform over SSH — provided you first identify which port, or you take the Ethernet down with it. The recommendation at the end is the honest one: build it into the health check, or buy a smart plug, because the next lockup will not wait for you to be awake.
